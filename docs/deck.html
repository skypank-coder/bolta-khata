Bolta Khata

The weekly Self-Help Group meeting, run by voice. Every entry is heard by the whole group before it is saved, and nobody can quietly change it later.

India Runs on Voice hackathon, Round 2
Theme: Livelihoods and small business
Working prototype: github.com/skypank-coder/bolta-khata

1

Problem: one woman writes the register, everyone else has to trust her

Over 9 crore rural women save and borrow through Self-Help Groups. Every week, 10 to 15 members sit together and hand over money. One member, the bookkeeper, writes it into a paper register. Often she is the only one who can read it.

  • Members cannot check what was written against their own name.
  • Errors and misuse surface months later as fights or missing money.
  • If the bookkeeper is absent or leaves, the records stop.
  • Banks want clean books before lending, so a messy register costs the whole group credit.
  • Apps like LokOS still depend on the one member who can read and type.
2

Solution: the meeting already happens out loud, so the record should too

  1. The leader says "meeting shuru karo". Members say their names for attendance.
  2. Each woman speaks her own entry: "Main Meena, sau rupaye bachat, paanch sau kist".
  3. The phone reads it back to everyone with her new balance and asks "sahi hai?".
  4. Only after "sahi hai" is it written. Loans need the leader's "manzoor" and enough group cash.
  5. "Meeting band karo" reads the day's totals and who paid nothing, then makes a bank report and a receipt for every member.

3

Why voice comes first, not as a feature on top

  • Most members cannot read a register or a screen, but every woman can speak and listen.
  • Reading each entry aloud turns a private notebook into a record the whole group checks together, in its own language.
  • A typing app would leave the same one woman in control. Voice gives every member her own way to check the accounts, and to ask "mera kitna jama hai?" at any time.
  • Hands-free: tap once and it listens, answers and listens again for the whole meeting. Every spoken question also has a big button for noisy rooms.
  • Nobody needs training: Sakhi Didi, an animated guide, shows every screen aloud; a coach under every reply says the exact words to say next.
4

What is built and working today

  • Hindi, Marathi and English, in Devanagari or Roman, mixed in one sentence.
  • Money as villages say it: dedh hazaar, dhai sau, saade teen hazaar, paune do hazaar, pachchis, paachshe.
  • Indian name matching: Sunita, Suneeta, Sunitha and सुनीता reach the same member. Two Sunitas? It asks which.
  • Members sit in a circle on screen, as on the durrie; a real group is set up by voice: "naya sadasya jodo: Pushpa Devi".
  • Guards: an overpaid loan is closed and the rest saved, large amounts confirmed twice, loans checked against group cash.
  • Tamper-evident ledger: each entry is locked to the previous one with SHA-256. A quiet edit turns the ledger red at that entry.
  • Mistakes are cancelled by reversal entries, never deleted.
  • Bank report (PDF), CSV, receipts read aloud or sent on WhatsApp.
  • Works offline as an installable app; saved online when there is signal.
  • Cash box counted by voice; any shortfall is announced and locked in.
  • Live product: website, mobile + PIN accounts, groups dashboard, offline-first cloud sync; 158 tests.

7

Launch plan: reach the first groups through the people they already trust

  1. Pilot: 30 groups in two districts (Hindi and Marathi), with an NGO partner and its community resource persons, who already visit these meetings.
  2. Side by side: paper register and app for three months, compared at every monthly check.
  3. One-click trial: any group or NGO can try a full group in seconds, guided by Sakhi; workers watch meetings live.
  1. Grow through cluster-level federations and State Rural Livelihood Missions: one resource person follows dozens of groups on one dashboard with join codes.
  2. Fit in: export to LokOS and bank formats, so it adds to existing systems instead of replacing them.
  3. Free for groups; paid per group per year by missions, NGOs or banks that want reliable records.
8

How we will know it works, and the main risk

Targets for the pilot

  • 80% of members can state their own balance without help.
  • Under 2% mismatch with the paper register.
  • A 15-member meeting recorded in under 30 minutes.
  • 70% of groups still using it after three months, by choice.

Main risk: mishearing money

A noisy room and strong accents can produce a wrong name or amount, and one wrong entry can break trust. That is why nothing is saved without the group's "sahi hai", large amounts are confirmed twice, and the hash chain keeps every correction visible.
