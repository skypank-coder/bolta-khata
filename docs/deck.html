Bolta Khata

The weekly Self-Help Group meeting, run by voice. Every member speaks her own entry, the phone reads it back to the whole group, and it is written only after the group says "sahi hai". After that, nobody can quietly change it.

1

Problem

One woman writes the register. Everyone else has to trust her.

Over 9 crore rural women in India save and borrow through about 90 lakh Self-Help Groups (SHGs) under DAY-NRLM. Every week, 10 to 15 members sit together and hand over money. One member, the bookkeeper, writes it into a paper register, and often she is the only one who can read it.

The meeting already happens out loud. The record does not.

  • No one can check: members cannot read what was written against their own name.
  • Errors surface late: mistakes and misuse appear months later, as fights or missing money.
  • Records stop: when the bookkeeper is absent or leaves, the register stops.
  • Cash does not match: the money in the box rarely matches the register, and audits catch it late.
  • Credit suffers: banks want clean books before lending, so a messy register costs the whole group.
  • Apps did not fix it: tools such as LokOS are typed by the same bookkeeper, so one woman stays in control.
2

Who has the problem

Five people at every meeting, each with a different pain

Member

Pays every week but cannot read the register. Learns her balance only when the bookkeeper tells her. Wants to hear it herself.

Bookkeeper

Writes everything, twice: paper and then an app. Is blamed for every mismatch. Wants the work to be shared and checked.

Leader

Approves loans and must keep the cash honest. Wants clear totals, who paid, who did not, and what is in the box.

NGO worker / CRP

Supports dozens of groups and visits each one rarely. Wants to see every group's health without travelling.

Bank and federation

Decides on credit for the group. Wants records nobody could have edited, and a clean meeting history.

One answer

A record made out loud, heard by all, confirmed by all, and locked. Bolta Khata serves all five from one phone at the meeting.

3

Solution

The meeting runs by voice, and the group confirms every entry

  1. The leader says "meeting shuru karo". Members say their names; each one's seat fills in the on-screen circle.
  2. Each woman speaks her entry: "main Meena, sau rupaye bachat, paanch sau kist".
  3. The phone reads it back to everyone with her new loan balance, and asks "sahi hai?".
  4. Only after "sahi hai" is it written and hash-locked. Loans need the leader's "manzoor" and enough group cash.
  5. "Meeting band karo" reads the totals and who paid nothing; the leader counts the cash by voice ("haath mein 20,100 hai") and the app says if anything is short.
  6. The bank report and a receipt for every member are produced automatically.

"मैं मीना, सौ रुपये बचत, पाँच सौ किस्त"

मीना देवी: बचत ₹100, किस्त ₹500। बाकी लोन ₹1,500। सही है?

"हाँ, सही है"

सही

The same in Marathi ("मी मीना, शंभर रुपये बचत") and English. Hindi is the default.

4

Why voice

Voice is the product, not a feature on top

  • Most members cannot read a register or a screen, but every woman can speak and listen.
  • Reading each entry aloud turns a private notebook into a record the whole group checks together, in its own language. A typing app leaves the same one woman in control.
  • Any member can ask "mera kitna jama hai?" at any meeting and hear her own balance.
  • Hands-free: tap once and it listens, answers and listens again for the whole meeting. Every spoken question also has a big button, for noisy rooms.
  • Nobody needs training: a coach under every reply shows the exact words to say next, help cards read themselves aloud, and Sakhi Didi, an animated guide, explains every screen by voice.
  • The speaker's own words light up as they are understood (name, amount, savings or repayment), so the group sees the phone understood before it answers.
5

Built for Indian speech

It understands money and names the way villages say them

  • Fractions: dedh hazaar (1,500), dhai sau (250), saade teen hazaar (3,500), paune do hazaar (1,750), sava sau (125).
  • All 99 irregular Hindi numbers, compounds like "ek hazaar do sau pachaas", English words, digits, ₹ and Indian digit grouping (1,25,000).
  • Marathi: पाचशे (500), दीड हजार (1,500), शंभर (100), case endings such as राधाला.
  • Devanagari or Roman, mixed in one sentence, with Hindi schwa deletion (कितना → kitnaa).
  • No false money: the last "do" in "Sunita ko do hazaar do" is a verb ("give"), and Marathi "हजर" (present) is not ₹1,000.
  • Names as recognisers hear them: Sunita, Suneeta, Sunitha and सुनीता reach the same member; Laxmi = Lakshmi.
  • Asks instead of guessing: with two Sunitas in a group, it asks "Sunita Kumari or Sunita Yadav?".
  • Rule-based on purpose: understanding runs on the phone in milliseconds, works offline, costs nothing and never invents an amount.
  • Tested at scale: 91,000 spoken Hindi amounts parsed back exactly; every on-screen example tested in all three languages.
6

Product: built and working today

A working product, not a mock-up

  • Meeting by voice: attendance, savings, repayments, loans with leader approval, corrections by reversal, balance questions, cash count, closing summary.
  • Tamper-evident ledger: every entry stores the SHA-256 of the one before; the server re-verifies the chain and every field on each sync.
  • Bank report (PDF), spreadsheet (CSV), and a receipt per member, read aloud or sent on WhatsApp.
  • Offline-first: the ledger lives on the phone and syncs when signal returns; installable as an app.
  • Live view: an NGO worker or bank officer follows a group with a 6-letter code and sees entries appear within seconds.
  • Website in Hindi, Marathi and English, with a meeting playing live.
  • Accounts: mobile number and PIN, like UPI; PINs stored only as scrypt hashes; lockout after wrong PINs.
  • One-click trial: a guest account and a 12-member group, ready in seconds, guided by Sakhi Didi.
  • Dashboard of every group a person runs or follows: cash, savings, loans out, last meeting, ledger intact.
  • One container: website, API and database start with "docker compose up". No keys.
7

Proof

Stress-tested, not demo-tested: 158 automated tests

  • 91,000 spoken Hindi amounts (10 to 99,999) parsed back exactly.
  • 600 random meetings of 45 noisy sentences each (Hinglish, Devanagari, half-sentences, silence, gibberish), checked after every sentence: the chain stays valid, cash never goes negative, no loan goes below zero, no money is written without a "yes", no member is added twice.
  • Whole spoken meetings in Hindi, Marathi and English through the real engine.
  • Every phrase the coach can suggest, tried in the state it is suggested.
  • API tests over real HTTP: sign-up, lockout, guest trial, sync, tampered or nonsensical entries refused, read-only followers.

Nine real bugs found by these tests, all fixed:

  • "tees" (30) was being saved as ₹23
  • पचहत्तर (75) was not understood
  • An overpaid loan could go below zero (now: loan closed, rest to savings)
  • An unanswered loan approval could freeze the meeting
  • "Which Sunita?" could ask forever
  • A sentence could become a new member's name
  • The same person could be added in English and in Hindi
  • Two quick taps could overwrite each other's entry
  • A view-only follower's phone could record entries
8

How it works

Simple, cheap and private by design

  • Speech to text in the browser (hi-IN, mr-IN, en-IN), keeping the recogniser's alternative guesses.
  • Transliteration of Devanagari to one Roman form, then the money parser and the name matcher.
  • Intent: start, attendance, entry, yes, no, approve, question, undo, cash count, new member, close.
  • Meeting state machine: a pure function (state, what was heard, ledger) → (new state, reply, entries), so every meeting can be replayed in a test.
  • Reply spoken in the chosen language by the phone's clearest voice, one sentence at a time, and shown on screen.
  • Ledger: append-only, SHA-256 hash chain, reversals instead of edits, cash counts locked in.
  • Storage: IndexedDB on the phone; SQLite on our server for accounts, groups and the cloud copy.
  • Sync: append-only; the server re-checks the chain and every field; if two phones split, the server's copy is kept and the phone is told.
  • Security: scrypt PINs, hashed session tokens in HttpOnly cookies, rate limits, JSON-only writes, read-only followers.
  • No AI API, no paid speech, no SMS: ₹0 cost per meeting today.

Third-party dependencies: risk plan

If a dependency gets costly or stops, the meeting does not

  • If browser recognition stops or is limited: the engine only needs text, so recognition is swapped behind one function: an on-device model (open-source Indian-language speech models) for fully offline use, or the government's Bhashini platform, or a commercial Indian speech API.
  • If speech becomes paid: at a typical ₹0.5–1.5 per audio minute (assumption) and about 10 minutes of speech per meeting, cost is ₹5–15 a meeting, ₹260–780 a group a year. On-device models bring this back to zero; the pilot price covers the middle case.
  • If there is no internet: typing and big buttons always work offline; the ledger is saved on the phone and syncs later.
  • If a phone has no good voice: the app picks the clearest one available, falls back to Hindi for Marathi, and always shows the text; each phone can choose its voice and speed.
  • If our server is down: meetings, reports and receipts still work on the phone; sync resumes when it returns.
  • If hosting costs rise: one container runs on any Docker host, including Indian clouds; SQLite needs no separate database service.
  • Mishearing money is the main product risk: nothing is saved without the group's "sahi hai", large amounts are confirmed twice, and every correction stays visible in the hash chain.

Go-to-market: channels and onboarding

How a group starts using it, in one meeting

  • Channel: community resource persons and NGO field staff, who already attend these meetings and are trusted by the group.
  • First meeting: the resource person opens the one-click trial or creates the group with its real cash; Sakhi Didi explains the screen aloud; members add themselves by voice ("naya sadasya: Pushpa Devi").
  • The leader learns three sentences ("meeting shuru karo", "sahi hai", "meeting band karo"); the coach on screen teaches the rest.
  • One phone per group is enough: the leader's or the resource person's.
  • Proof for funders: the live view and dashboard show the NGO or mission every group's meetings without travelling.
  • Pilot targets: 80% of members can state their own balance without help; under 2% mismatch with the paper register; a 15-member meeting recorded in under 30 minutes; 70% of groups still using it by choice after three months.
  • Pricing: free for groups; ₹600 per group per year for institutions (proposed), free during pilots.
  • Why they say yes: fewer disputes, cleaner books for bank linkage, cash checked every meeting, and no training cost.
